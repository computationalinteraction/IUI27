About the tutorial

This full-day, hands-on tutorial teaches you to formulate HCI problems computationally, choose an appropriate computational method, and implement it on real interaction problems. We open with an overview of computational interaction as a problem-solving approach, then cover three topics in depth: learning from users, modeling users, and assisting users.

Each session proceeds from a concrete HCI problem to a technical formulation of that problem and then to the methods that solve it. You implement those methods yourself in Python notebooks rather than observing a demonstration. An informal breakout over lunch covers how the material transfers to your own teaching and research.

You must be able to program in Python and should bring a laptop. We assume no prior exposure to the specific methods covered.

Sessions

An opening overview followed by three in-depth sessions, each pairing a lecture with a hands-on exercise.

  1. 1

    Overview

    Computational interaction as a way of thinking, establishing the reasoning shared across its methods.

  2. 2

    Learning from Users

    Adapting a system to an individual user from costly and indirect feedback, through multi-armed bandits, Bayesian optimization, and reinforcement learning from human feedback.

  3. 3

    Modeling Users

    Predicting human behavior to evaluate interfaces before deployment and adapt them at runtime, through cognitive, data-driven, generative, and reinforcement learning models.

  4. 4

    Assisting Users

    Choosing assistant actions without knowing the user’s reward function, through reinforcement learning, two-agent games, and generative agents.

Materials

You keep everything used during the session: lecture slides for every session in PDF and PPTX, lecture notes accompanying each module, hands-on exercises as Python notebooks with reference solutions, and further instructional material for extending the topics beyond the session. All materials are released under permissive licenses (MIT for code, CC-BY for slides), so you can retain, adapt, and redistribute them.
